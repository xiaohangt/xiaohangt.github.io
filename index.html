Research overview

Hi — I'm Xiaohang. I'm a Student Researcher at Google DeepMind (London) and a PhD candidate at UCL advised by Ilija Bogunovic. I have also been fortunate to work closely with Yaodong Yang.

My central question is how a learning system can keep improving from signals it generates itself, the way people learn most new skills: by practicing, judging their own attempts, and refining, rather than by following an exhaustive answer key. External supervision such as human labels and preference annotations is expensive, finite, and tends to cap a model at the level of whoever provides it; self-improvement lets learning continue past that limit, by interacting with the open world.

Self-play in games. In extensive-form games, I study double oracle methods that grow a pool of strategies by repeatedly adding best responses to the current equilibrium, converging to a Nash equilibrium of the full game. This includes regret-minimizing double oracle (RMDO, ICML 2023) and its sample-efficient extension with adaptive strategy-pool expansion (AdaDO, JMLR).

Imagined self-play for robust decision-making. When the opponent cannot be queried, the agent plays against an adversary it imagines from data. The Adversarially Robust Decision Transformer (ARDT, NeurIPS 2024) conditions a sequence model on worst-case returns, and CART (NeurIPS 2025 Reliable ML Workshop) extends this to stochastic games.

Self-play alignment for LLMs. I treat preference optimization as a game that a language model plays against itself. RSPO (ICML 2026) adds plug-and-play regularization to self-play alignment with provable convergence to the Nash equilibrium, and RMOD (ICLR 2026) brings the same game view to controlled decoding (test-time alignment), where the opponent is instead an adversarial weighting of the alignment objectives, so the decoded response stays robust to the worst-case objective.

Self-distillation for diffusion LLMs. For diffusion language models, I post-train the denoiser on signals it already produces, avoiding unstable likelihood-ratio estimates. wd1 (ICLR 2026) is a ratio-free weighted policy optimization for reasoning, and GDSD (NeurIPS 2026) recasts RL as distilling a reward-guided version of the denoiser back into itself, making it completely likelihood-free and ELBO-free.

Before the PhD, I obtained my master's degree in Data Science at UCL and a bachelor's degree in Math at Sun Yat-sen University (SYSU).
